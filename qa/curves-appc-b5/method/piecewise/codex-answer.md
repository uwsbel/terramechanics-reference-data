**I recommend piecewise linear calibration between verified scale lines, including a frame whose axis value is established. Apply this consistently, without a spacing threshold.** This defines how we read the drawing; it does not prove how the drafter positioned every symbol.

On FC-6B and FC-6D, I would assign the left frame **x = 0**, recording that value as inferred. The first lower-panel symbols sit on that frame, and both readers identify them as zero-displacement readings. Excluding it gives a cleaner fit by sacrificing the origin. Conversely, the current full-span affine fit compromises both the origin and the 50 line. Local interpolation respects both. [AM-68-1 Vol. II, Fig. C-6B, printed C-18, PDF 132](corpus/1968-vol2/images/p0132.png); [Fig. C-6D, printed C-20, PDF 134](corpus/1968-vol2/images/p0134.png).

The strongest counterargument is Claude’s observation that FC-6D’s symbols follow nearly constant displacement steps under the interior-grid affine map. That suggests plotting against a uniform ruler, but does not establish it: the original displacement sequence is unavailable, symbol placement is imperfect, and the frame symbols still require an exception. I prefer an explicit graphical-scale convention over selecting anchors for their fit residual. [Claude’s FC-6D reasoning](extractions/claude/curves-appc-b3/FC-6D/notes.md).

My proposed protocol is:

- **Mapping:** Interpolate each axis between adjacent verified lines at the point’s location, retaining the fitted line tilts. Use the axis’s transformed coordinates for logarithmic scales. Uniform grids naturally give an affine result. Missing lines are not knots; interpolate between the nearest genuine marks. A frame becomes a knot only with a supported value. Misplaced text and synthetic `predicted` lines do not establish scale positions, consistent with [D-024](docs/decisions.csv). Flag extrapolation beyond the supported range.
- **Bounds and residuals:** Propagate the point’s pixel interval and both bracketing lines’ position intervals through the map. Include line-location error measured against independent eye checks or withheld image bands. If an interval crosses a knot, evaluate both segments. Zero residual at interpolation knots is guaranteed and earns no accuracy credit. Retain the global affine residual as a drafting diagnostic, rather than adding it to every corrected point as the current [tool](tools/calibrate_axes.py) does. These remain reading bounds conditional on the local-scale convention. Report affine versus piecewise differences separately as correlated calibration sensitivity, especially for initial stiffness.
- **Comparison and canonical choice:** Require identical verified knot identities and coverage. Compare maps throughout the actual plot domain, including every knot and interval, rather than only four corners. Replace fitted-anchor RMS ranking with independent line-position validation over the same evidence and domain; retain the existing first-submission tie-break when validation cannot distinguish them. Never select a map because its curve looks smoother or its inferred stiffness looks preferable. Preserve the point pairing, one-sided coverage, and blind adjudication rules of [§4 and D-023](docs/CURVE-READING.md).
- **Existing readings:** Keep submissions immutable and retain both readers’ pixels. Independently recover any omitted frame anchors, then write versioned derivatives under `qa/<batch>/rederived/`, regenerate values, bounds and SI outputs, and rerun reconciliation. Preserve valid ink and identity decisions by source and sequence; recompute numerical agreement and canonical selection. This extends [D-024’s re-derivation rule](docs/decisions.csv).

I would change my mind if original numerical records, explicit plotting instructions, or known intermediate values showed that symbols followed a uniform scale independently of the drawn grid. Until then, piecewise is the more reproducible reading convention. Its changes in slope at grid boundaries must not themselves be interpreted as soil behavior.
