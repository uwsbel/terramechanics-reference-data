Open questions

This data set is a first step in a longer journey, not a finished product. The numbers come from scanned reports more than half a century old, read twice by independent agents, reconciled by rule, and sampled by student auditors. That process leaves questions we could not settle, and a few we chose not to spend more time on yet. They are listed here so that a user knows where the soft spots are, and so that anyone who wants to help can pick one up. If you can answer one, open an issue or a pull request in the repository, or write to the maintainers; a better scan, a printed copy of a report, or an hour with a figure on your screen is enough to close several of them.

A reading uncertainty on this site is the uncertainty of our reading of the printed figure, not the uncertainty of the experiment. Nothing below changes that distinction.

Reading the figures

  1. Trace samples in gaps. A sampled point on a plotted curve should sit on the line’s own ink, not on a symbol or in the white gap between a dash and a symbol. A mechanical check now removes samples that fail this (it was validated blind on 400 samples: 98 percent of what it removes is rightly removed), but it finds only about half of the failures. The misses are at curve ends on the frame or a grid line, and at junctions where a symbol’s outline fuses with the line. About 300 samples it could not decide are kept and marked for review. See qa/adjudication-2026-10-28-inkrule/review.md and tools/check_trace_ink.py.
  2. Symbols merged with grid lines or with each other. For 22 points of the first audit wave, two rounds of blind re-reading could not say where the symbol’s center is (8 undecided, 14 still split within a pixel). They are in the data with widened bounds and the status pending-review. See qa/adjudication-2026-10-27/review.md.
  3. Which point of a triangle the 1968 plotter registered. A plotted triangle has two defensible centers, its centroid and the middle of its bounding box, about one sixth of its height apart. We accept both and carry the difference in the bound (docs/CURVE-READING.md, rule 8). A page of the report or of the plotter’s manual that states the convention would tighten every marker.
  4. Grain-size bands that merge. On the Appendix A grain-size charts the average curve and the two range curves run together over parts of their length, and neither reader could tell them apart there. See insights/1968-vol2-appendix-a-grain-size-curves-against-their-tables.md.
  5. Residual labels on log and reciprocal axes. One item of the axis transform work (D-027) remains: the site labels residuals in printed units rather than in the transformed coordinate. Values and verdicts are unaffected. The other two items are done (2026-11-18): the canonical files now record where the other reading lies on the page, and audit packets draw it there, instead of placing it with a map fitted to the kept reading.

The printed sources themselves

  1. Seven cells of Table 2-2 (Volume I, printed 2-14). The page is a 200 dpi bilevel scan on which 5, 6 and 8 print alike. The cells carry both readings. A better scan or a printed copy of AM-68-1 Volume I would settle them. See qa/pages-s2-b1/review.md.
  2. Table 2-3 against the Appendix A specifications. The summary table of Volume I and the per-soil specification sheets of Volume II disagree on six friction angles (soils 1, 3, 4, 7, 11, 12) and on the gradations of four soils; 26 cells differ in all. Both Volcalite reprints are misprinted and the first printing is right. We record both values and do not know which the authors used downstream. See qa/specifications/table23-vs-appendix-a.md.
  3. The Table 2-3 modulus. It appears to be IITRI’s small-strain wave modulus, interpolated in relative density from two tested densities. This is an inference from the Appendix B plots, not a statement in the report.
  4. Computer plots against their printed fits. On the Appendix C computer plots (Figs. C-8 to C-15) the drawn least-squares lines depart from the fit parameters printed on the same plot by up to 96 psf, while the printed parameters reproduce from the data sheets. We do not know why the plotter drew the lines where it did. See insights/1968-vol2-appendix-c-computer-plots-against-printed-fits.md.
  5. Relations between records that could not be resolved. Five items of the Volume II figure index carry the relation unresolved (the Soil No. 10 specification table, printed A-71, PDF 72, and Figs. C-12 to C-15): each appears to repeat material printed elsewhere, but the evidence does not say whether it is the same item or a derived one (for example Fig. C-12 against data sheet C-4B). They are counted once, on the side of caution. (Corrected 2026-11-05: this item said 31 rows of the experiment index; that index has no unresolved relation, only same-as and derived-from.)
  6. Setup fields. Many records do not yet carry a complete setup (apparatus, rate, sample preparation) because the report prints it once for a family and the index has not been completed with those fields (setup_status on each record).
  7. The load axis of Figure 2-34. Its title reads Load - Lbs. x 100 while the printed slope (54 lb per inch) and intercept (140 lb) of the fit drawn on it, and the same test LPT 87 on Figure 2-48 (plain Lbs.), only make sense in plain pounds. The data record the axis numbers as pounds and note the title; a reader who knows what the x 100 meant is welcome. The fit line’s intercept read on the axis numbers is 139 lb, beside the printed 140 lb (qa/curves-s2-b1/review.md).
  8. The authors’ straight lines. The fit lines (Slope = ...) and the bearing-capacity line on Figures 2-32 to 2-34 are read like any curve but have no experiment record yet, so they sit in the batch files, not in data/, until the index gains derived-series records for them.
  9. The first inch of Figure 2-48 (Volume I, printed 2-83). Its 0 to 1 in interval is drawn about 233 px tall against 304 to 316 px for every other inch. Both readings take the grid as drawn (the bottom frame is 0 in). If the zero line was meant to sit at the regular spacing, every value below 1 in shifts by up to about 0.25 in. The same test, LPT 87, is printed on Figure 2-34 with a regular first inch, which a comparison of the two printings could use (qa/curves-s2-b2/review.md).
  10. Grid lines beyond the last printed label. On Figures 2-32, 2-34 and 2-45 the frame runs past the last labelled line, and the unlabelled lines there (penetration 10 and 11 in, 7 to 9 in; load 275 and 300 lb) are valued by the even grid spacing. The two readings differ there by at most 0.08 in, because one anchored those lines and the other extrapolated; the record values rest on the anchored lines.

The audit

  1. Points checked by one reviewer only. Every sampled point is meant to be checked by two students. Where one of the pair has not returned a chunk, the point stands as checked by one person, and its row in qa/audits.csv says so. Nothing in the pipeline waits for the second return; a later wave can re-deal such points if a user needs two signatures on a figure.
  2. Scan of the sample, not the population. The audits cover every disputed point and a sample of the agreed ones, sized by the square root of the points in a figure. An agreed point outside the sample rests on the agreement of two readings made separately and on the rules, not on a person.

Scope

  1. Not yet read. Most Volume I curves. In Section II, the eight load-penetration figures 2-29, 2-32 to 2-34 and 2-45 to 2-48 and the three DPT signatures of Fig. 2-54 (the dynamic penetrometer) are read; the other load-penetration figures, the pressure parameter plots, the DPT correlation, rate series and density control are not, nor Tables 1-1, 2-1 and 2-2. Of Sections III to V, three impact tests, two model drop tests and the run tables of Fig. 4-6a (drag) and Table 5-3 (model drop) are read; the other impact and drag records are indexed only.
  2. Not yet extracted sources. The Apollo 15 and 16 penetrometer tables and the WES M-71-10 reports are acquired and registered but not yet turned into records; 13 further leads are acquired-with-caveat and await a rights decision each.
  3. Later simulants. Whether data on GRC-1, JSC-1A and their successors enter the first release is undecided (D-015).

Points counted twice

  1. A curve’s end recorded by both readings. Where the other reading’s last (or first) sample lies just beyond the canonical end of a trace, within both readings’ bounds of that end, the comparison keeps it as a point of its own, so the end is recorded twice, a fraction of a pixel apart. Applied to the batches read before 2026-11-19, the rule that drops such a duplicate would remove 193 points (159 verified on the page by the other agent, 34 one-sided) from the records; the values are not wrong, only counted twice. The rule is used from the dynamic penetrometer batch on (tools/diff_curves.py --end-dedup); whether to apply it to the earlier records, some of whose points the students have audited, is open.

Two printed values for one quantity

  1. The touchdown velocities of NASA Tests No. 1 and 2. Table 5-T3 (Volume I, printed 5-45, PDF 325) is in the records b68-mdrop-t5-3-rs and b68-mdrop-t5-3-ss since 2026-11-20, every bed’s relative density, unit weight and friction angle with it, and the record pages of the two tests give its values. Its velocities “were calculated based on the exact time interval between the instant of model release to the instant of ground contact” (printed 5-44, PDF 324). The insets of the strut-load figures print others: -114.93 in./sec. for Test No. 1 (Fig. 5-25, printed 5-50, PDF 330; the table gives -114.9) and, on a degraded scan, -60.55 and 83.75 in./sec. for Test No. 2 (Fig. 5-32, printed 5-57, PDF 337; the table gives -60.5 and 83.5, and 83.75 does not round to 83.5). Our arithmetic, against |V_v| = g tau (the speed of a free fall from rest over tau) within the rounding of both printed numbers: with the report’s g for Earth, 32.2 ft/s^2 (glossary, printed 2-2, PDF 27), three of the four vertical drops are consistent with it and none of the four with a horizontal velocity; with standard gravity, all four vertical drops and one angled drop (Test No. 3). The two values of g differ by 0.08 percent, about the rounding of a printed tau, so at this precision the verdicts depend on the g used. That shows consistency, not the method, which the report does not state (qa/tables-s5-b1/arithmetic.md). Both values are in the records with their sources; which one a simulation starts from is a study’s choice. How the inset values were obtained, and the method behind the angled drops’ velocities, are open.

How to help

  1. Pick an item, say which, and send what you found with the page, figure and pixel or printed value, so it can enter the record with a citation like every other number here.
  2. If you own or can scan a printed copy of AM-68-1 (Bendix, 1968, both volumes) at 300 dpi or better, items 6 and 7 close almost by themselves.
  3. If you ran, or know who ran, these tests or their plotter, items 3 and 9 are yours.

Every item above names its evidence file in the repository; the decision log (docs/decisions.csv) records what was decided about it and when.
