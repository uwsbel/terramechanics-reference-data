D-024 2026-10-14 Five method questions from batches curves-appc-b3 to -b5: calibration of gridless figures, triangle centers, envelope identity, a printed legend error, re-deriving values after a tool fix residual rule as is or limited to printed anchors; bounding-box center or centroid; line or legend shape for identity; keep or correct a printed legend; re-read or re-derive A calibration residual counts only against printed marks (detected grid lines or measured ticks); residuals over lines predicted from the frame show internal consistency and cannot win the canonical choice, so a batch may name the canonical reading with its reason (overlays.csv canonical, canonical_reason); a reader who saved a tick map is re-derived on it so both compete on printed marks. Triangle center: the centroid of the reconstructed triangle; until the 1968 registration point is established the bound in the apex direction includes the bounding-box midpoint (height/6); earlier readings with mixed conventions are to be normalized by a documented re-derivation (open). Envelope identity: neither line nor shape decides alone; the data-sheet peaks of the same tests, where they distinguish the candidates, settle it (corroboration from another depiction, not independent validation). A printed legend error is corrected by a canonical-stage series rename with its evidence (series-ids.csv), the submissions and *_printed fields faithful to the print. Values re-derived from a submission’s own pixels and calibration after a tool fix are sound and kept under qa//rederived/.
D-025 2026-10-14 One affine map per panel, or a piecewise map between the drawn lines, on hand-drafted grids that are not uniform? affine over all lines; affine over the labelled lines only; piecewise linear between verified lines Piecewise linear between verified scale lines on every panel (no threshold: on a uniform grid it equals the affine map), in the axis’s transformed coordinates for log scales, keeping each line’s tilt. A frame is a knot only with a supported value (on FC-6B and FC-6D the left frame is x = 0, recorded as inferred: the first lower-panel symbols sit on it); predicted lines and misplaced text are never knots. Bounds propagate the point’s pixel interval and the position intervals of its two bracketing lines, with line-location error from independent checks; the global affine residual becomes a diagnostic of the drafting and is no longer added to every point. The two readings’ maps are compared at every knot and interval over the plot, not only at four corners, and the canonical reading is chosen by independent line-position validation (tie to the first submission). The affine-minus-piecewise difference is reported as a correlated calibration sensitivity (initial stiffness above all); slope changes at grid lines are not soil behaviour. Existing readings: pixels kept, values re-derived under qa//rederived/, reconciliation rerun, ink and identity decisions carried by source and sequence. To be implemented in tools/calibrate_axes.py, diff_curves.py and build_observations.py as one step, then all batches re-derived (open).
D-026 2026-10-14 D-025 as implemented: how is a reader’s line verified against the page, which reading is canonical when the ink cannot separate the maps, and what happens to earlier decisions when the canonical reading changes? tie: first submission, previous canonical reader, denser sampling, affine residual; decisions: re-adjudicate everything, carry by neighbourhood, carry through the pairing; line check: median distance, ink-line fit over the line’s length Tie: first submission (Claude), within 0.25 px of mean line distance from the ink; recomputable from the submissions, the page and the recovered lines, unlike the previous reader, and not a measure of sampling effort or of the retired residual. A reader’s line is a knot only when the ink supports its whole length (ink at a quarter of its positions, at least 3, over a third of its length, the ink’s fitted line within 2 px of it at both ends; a tick on its stubs); lines a reader left out or misplaced are recovered from that reading’s own neighbouring lines and recorded in qa//knots.csv (29 lines and two frames). Coverage: equal lines, then the ink; a strict superset wins when its shared lines are as close; incomparable coverage goes to review. Bounds move the two bracketing lines to the ends of their intervals exactly. Exclusions are applied to both readings before matching and leave a break in a trace; a swap takes the other reading’s whole record (source -interp for an interpolated trace value); a resolution on a paired marker or feature follows the symbol when the canonical reading changes, a trace verification stays with its exact sample; the other reading’s samples in gaps of the canonical trace are kept as one-sided. Maps compared along every line of either reading and every interval midline. The new and newly exposed items go to the blind two-round adjudication.
D-027 2026-10-15 How do the curve-reading tools handle log and reciprocal axes (Appendix A grain-size curves on log paper, density charts with unit weight on a reciprocal axis)? (a) readers pass logarithms or reciprocals as anchor values (the old rule 5); (b) the tool owns the transform: declared per axis, anchors keep printed values, every map works in the plotting coordinate q = T(value); (c) resample the scan into linear coordinates (b). calibrate_axes.py detect –x-transform/–y-transform (linear, log10, reciprocal) records the transform; detect predicts unlabelled lines at their place in q; the affine fit, the D-025 knots, line recovery and the interpolation of traces in diff_curves work in q; values, bounds and SI are in the printed unit; the read bound is the larger half of the interval mapped back from q (symmetric and conservative), and values whose halves differ by more than 10 percent are counted. Two readings with different transforms are not compared until the axis type is settled from the page. Minor lines of log paper are knots only when their identity is unambiguous.
D-028 2026-10-15 Should the data label experiments as calibration data or validation data? (a) label each experiment’s role (element tests for calibration, penetration tests for validation); (b) keep the data agnostic: record facts any split needs and leave the roles to each study (b). Almost any test can calibrate or validate; the role is a choice made in a particular study for a particular claim with a particular model. The data records, per experiment, what kind of test it is (element, boundary-value, derived series, summary), what was measured and how well (setup completeness, response, uncertainty, repeat-test scatter), and how tests depend on each other (same soil, state, laboratory; same-as, derived-from), plus notes on what a test can and cannot pin down for a given kind of model. Example splits are documented with the benchmark, not in the data.
