4. Bed preparation is in Section II (outside this batch): AM-68-1 Vol. I, Fig. 2-61, printed 2-105, PDF 130 (hopper)

Given in numbers:

1. Soil bin about 10 ft long, 3 ft wide, false bottom giving soil depths 11 to 12 in; two identical bins (text
   printed 3-5, PDF 146; Fig. 3-2, printed 3-6, PDF 147).
2. Carriage: bungee (rubber cord) acceleration, constant speed over a 6 ft test run within about 9 ft of bed,
   spring and damper deceleration (printed 3-5 to 3-7, PDF 146 to 148). With the 1/6-scale pad: accelerations up to
   3 g's, constant velocities up to 18 ft/s, decelerations up to about 6 g's, vertical and drag forces up to 250 lb,
   penetration up to 6 in (printed 3-7, PDF 148). Drive motor 3 hp after modification (printed 3-4, PDF 145).
3. Footpad: 1/6-scale LM footpad of steel with full-scale contours (printed 3-13, PDF 154), diameters 6 and 4 inch
   (Fig. 4-2, printed 4-5, PDF 190; column D of each table); can be locked (printed 3-15, PDF 156); pitch alpha
   recorded per run.
4. Control modes: constant penetration with the air-cylinder piston locked, or constant vertical load with the
   cylinder pressurized (printed 3-13, PDF 154). Planned VH 1.5 to 15 fps, depths 0.5 to 4 inch, vertical pressures
   1, 3, 5 psi (Fig. 4-2). Only constant-penetration results are tabulated.
5. Density: Dr and gamma per soil from the dynamic penetrometer reading gp (printed 4-7, PDF 192; gp-bar column).
6. Recording: nine oscillograph channels, C.E.C. 5-114 oscillograph, manual reduction (Fig. 3-11); 1/10 second
   timing marks and per-channel sensitivities printed on the record (Fig. 4-5, printed 4-6, PDF 191); each function
   read at three positions over 40 in of displacement and the midpoint reading used (printed 4-7, PDF 192).
7. Gravity: laboratory, Earth gravity; no statement of reduced gravity for this rig (inferred from the absence of
   any; the IITRI vacuum/subgravity work is in Appendix E, printed 3-44, PDF 185).

Not found:

1. Footpad mass and the moving mass of carriage, strut and pad.
2. The pad profile in numbers (only the full-scale drawing, Fig. 3-26, whose dimensions are unreadable).
3. Whether the pad was free to pitch or locked in each tabulated run.
4. Bed preparation per soil and bed depth per test in Section IV itself (Section II has the hopper and density).
5. Time histories for any run except 2-20; side load Fs is recorded (Fig. 4-5) but not tabulated.
6. Recorder paper speed or frequency response.

   printed 4-25, PDF 210; Fig. 4-22, printed 4-26, PDF 211 (lower panel).

Given in numbers:

1. Soil bed 3 ft by 8 ft by 2 ft deep (printed 3-17, PDF 158); a new, larger bin is mentioned after modification
   (printed 3-20, PDF 161) without dimensions in what I read.
2. Impact angle from vertical (0) to about 80 deg; the body is free after the piston clears the discharge port
   (printed 3-17, 3-20, PDF 158, 161). 24 in aluminum square impact bar with lead weights (printed 3-20, PDF 161).
3. Impacting mass (footpad, load cell and bar) minimum about 32 lb, maximum about 50 lb (printed 3-22, PDF 163);
   program values 31, 41 and 51 pounds (Fig. 4-17); legend values M1 = 31, 41, 51 lbs. (Fig. 4-21).
4. Velocity: above 18 ft/s possible (printed 3-22, PDF 163); program 1, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 fps (Fig. 4-17); the

5. Footpad: 1/6-scale instrumented steel pad, ball socket allowing +/-30 deg, pitch and roll rotary pots (printed
   3-23, PDF 164); diameter 6.17 inches in the plots (Figs. 4-19 to 4-22); 6 and 4 inch in the program (Fig. 4-17).
6. Load capacity 15,000 lb vertical, 500 lb horizontal; stroke by slide wire potentiometer; accelerometer on top of
   the load cell (printed 3-22, PDF 163). Displacement is the only measure of velocity (printed 4-7, PDF 192).
7. Beds per run: Dr 0, 0.45, 0.65 (legend, Fig. 4-19); text calls them RS loose, intermediate, dense.
8. Recording: C.E.C. 5-124 oscillograph or Minneapolis-Honeywell 8100 tape (Fig. 3-19), manual reduction (printed
   3-23, PDF 164). Time base in milliseconds on Fig. 4-19.

Not found:

1. Measured impact velocity for each plotted run (only "about 6 fps").
2. Impacting mass for runs 10 and 12 (Fig. 4-19 legend gives none).
3. Unit weight of the impact beds and how they were prepared; size of the larger bin.

5. Whether "Test Nc. II-1" of Fig. 4-22 is run 4 of Figs. 4-19 to 4-21.

   PDF 325; histories AM-68-1 Vol. I, Figs. 5-22 to 5-39, printed 5-47 to 5-64, PDF 327 to 344.

Given in numbers:

1. Scale factors, full scale over model (Table 5-T1): length 6; time 6; angular velocity 1/6; linear velocity 1.0;
   linear acceleration 1/6; mass 6^3; mass density 1.0; mass moment of inertia 6^5; force 6^2; spring rate 6.0;
   coefficient of friction 1.0. Inferred: with length and time both scaled by 6, a model dropped at Earth gravity
   represents the full-scale vehicle at lunar gravity (acceleration ratio 1/6); the pages I read do not state the
   gravity of the test in words.
2. Model mass and inertia (table on PDF 302): total weight 68.4 Lbs. (full scale 14,760 lbs. on Fig. 5-9);
   Io(Xb) 1.6022, Io(Yb) 1.2880, Io(Zb) 1.3402, Io(Ys) 0.09917, Io(Zs) 0.09914 slug ft.^2; initial CG height
   23.90 inches; primary strut assembly weight 1.82 lbs. with its CG 14.66 inches from the upper hinge; footpad to
   centerline radius 27.928 inches.
3. Struts: crushable aluminum honeycomb (1/8 in cell, 0.0007 and 0.001 in foil), design load-stroke steps in
   Fig. 5-10 (primary 167 lb then 333 lb, stroke limit 3.875 in; secondary compression 139 lb, tension steps 13,
   42, 139, 167 lb); crush tolerances +/-10 percent and measured initial dynamic primary load 200 to 225 lb
   (printed 5-17, 5-18, PDF 297, 298). Pad stops limit pad angle to 24 deg; pads tilted up 2 deg at touchdown
   (printed 5-9, PDF 289).
4. Facility: soil bed 18 ft long, 12 ft wide, up to 5 ft deep; six degree of freedom drop rig (printed 5-22, PDF
   302). Initial attitude: zero pitch and roll and zero angular rates; yaw 45 deg (2-2 landing) for the detailed
   correlation drops (printed 5-44, PDF 324); -22 1/2 deg yaw for the rigid-surface drops (printed 5-35, PDF 315).
5. Soil beds for detailed correlation: placed by the large hopper of Fig. 2-63 in about four-inch layers; loose
   beds not further handled; RS intermediate rolled after each pass with a 63-pound lawn roller, 18 inches in
   diameter and 24 inches wide, eight passes per 24-inch width; bed depth 18 inches; DPT readings after each drop;
   soil removed before a new bed (printed 5-43, 5-44, PDF 323, 324). Bed values per drop: Dr, gamma, phi (Tables
   5-T2, 5-T3). Gross correlation RS dense: "10 rolls per two-inch layer" (Table 5-T2, drop 22).
6. Velocities: Table 5-T2 nominal VV, VH (unit not printed; the text uses feet/second); Table 5-T3 computed from
   the release-to-touchdown time tau, in In./Sec. (Test 1: -114.9, 0, tau .298 s; Test 2: -60.5, 83.5, tau .177 s).
7. Rigid surface: spiked pads with rubber cushions, axial spring rate 4380 pounds/inch, lateral 1725 pounds/inch,
   about equal in weight to the regular pad; surface 3/4-inch fir plywood on tables, tilted 10 deg (printed 5-33,
   PDF 313).
8. Recording: 37 sensors; for detailed correlation 13 signals on two 7-channel tapes and 24 on two oscillographs;
   a release pulse on the CG X accelerometer gives a common time base; tape signals filtered and digitized every
   1/1000 second, reduced by McDonnell Automation Center to 17 output parameters (printed 5-38, 5-39, 5-41, 5-43,
   PDF 318, 319, 321, 323). Rigid-surface drops: 30 of 37 signals on two oscillographs (printed 5-35, PDF 315).

Not found:

1. Model footpad diameter, profile and mass as labeled values (Fig. 5-9 prints many dimensions without saying
   which is the pad; the full-scale pad is 37 in, so 6.17 in is implied by the scale but not printed in these pages).
2. Strut friction and damping beyond the remark that measured loads exceed capsule loads; secondary strut stroke
   limits.
3. Pad-soil friction; bed depth and preparation for the gross-correlation drops (types D, E, G) beyond "10 rolls"
   and "Rolled"; soil values for drops 1 to 9, 14 and 15 (dashes).
4. Load cell constants A, E, a, b, c and alpha needed to apply the force equations (printed 5-40, 5-41, PDF 320,
   321) are defined but not given numerically.
5. Histories for any drop other than NASA Tests 1 and 2; the rigid-surface histories are in "a separate data
   package" (printed 5-35, PDF 315).
6. A statement of the gravity of the test (see item 1 of "Given").
