## Where each setup fact is printed

1. Rig name and purpose: Subscale Drag Test Equipment (SSD), a Bendix rig from report MM-65-2, modified for
   this study; the footpad moves horizontally at constant velocity at either constant depth or constant
   vertical load. AM-68-1 Vol. I, text printed 3-1 and 3-2, PDF 142 and 143; Fig. 3-1, printed 3-3, PDF 144.
2. Soil bin: "approximately 10 feet long and 3 feet wide with a built-in false bottom that provides soil
   depths of 11 to 12 inches"; two identical bins side by side, one prepared while the other is tested.
   Text printed 3-5, PDF 146; Fig. 3-2 label "Soil Bin 10' Long and 3' Wide", printed 3-6, PDF 147.
3. Motion: acceleration over distance "a" by rubber bungee cords, constant velocity by a cam rod pulled by a
   friction drive wheel (3 hp motor, speed regulator, flywheel), deceleration by springs and a damper
   cylinder; all three phases within a nine-foot length of the bed, six feet of it at constant speed. Text
   printed 3-4 to 3-7, PDF 145 to 148; Figs. 3-3 and 3-4, printed 3-8, PDF 149. Text printed 4-4 (PDF 189)
   says the constant-velocity part covers approximately five feet. Rig limits with the 1/6-scale pad: up to
   3 g acceleration, 18 ft/s, about 6 g deceleration, 250 lb vertical and drag force, six inches of
   penetration (printed 3-7, PDF 148).
4. Depth control: "For constant footpad penetration tests, the piston is mechanically locked to the air
   cylinder in the desired position" (printed 3-13, PDF 154). The depth d of each run is in the d column;
   Fig. 4-4 (printed 4-6, PDF 191) defines d as the distance from the soil surface to the bottom of the
   footpad at zero pitch angle, A_ft as the total frontal area and A_pt as the total vertical area.
5. Footpad: the 1/6-scale footpad is steel, with inside and outside contours simulating the full-size LM pad;
   the strut simulates the lower end of the LM strut (printed 3-13, PDF 154). Fig. 3-10 (printed 3-15,
   PDF 156): ball pivot, two linear potentiometers about eight inches above the pad on push rods at 45
   degrees for pitch and roll, an antiyaw link, and the option to lock the pad in a fixed attitude "for
   certain tests". Diameter D per run in each table (6 or 4 inch); Fig. 4-2 (printed 4-5, PDF 190) lists
   6- and 4-inch diameters. No dimensioned drawing of either pad is printed.
6. Load measurement: three-axis strut load cell, Figs. 3-5 to 3-7 and text printed 3-9 to 3-13, PDF 150 to
   154; cross-sensitivity checks, Figs. 3-8 and 3-9, printed 3-14, PDF 155; recording chain, Fig. 3-11,
   printed 3-16, PDF 157 (oscillograph records reduced by hand).
7. Reduction: the functions were read at three positions covering 40 inches of travel and the midpoint
   reading was used (printed 4-7, PDF 192). Each table row is that one reading; no time histories are
   tabulated. Fig. 4-5 (printed 4-6, PDF 191) is the record of run 2-20, the only record printed.
8. Bed state: D_r and bulk density "were determined from dynamic penetrometer tests (g_p) of the soil bed"
   (printed 4-7, PDF 192). g_p is printed per run; D_r and gamma are one value per table except Fig. 4-6b,
   Fig. 4-6c (gamma per run) and Fig. 4-16 (two D_r values).
9. Bed preparation (Section II): soils air-dried, moisture 0.2 to 0.3 percent, occasionally 0.6 (printed
   2-103, PDF 128); loose beds by the hopper method, Dr about 0, with the hopper shown over the subscale drag
   bin (printed 2-104, PDF 129; Fig. 2-61, printed 2-105, PDF 130); intermediate beds of 2- to 4-inch hopper
   layers rolled with a lawn roller (printed 2-110, PDF 135); dense beds in the drag and impact bins by the
   lawn roller (printed 2-113, PDF 138); cinder soils (RS, PS, RSM) moved by the vacuum system degraded, LSM
   and RC2 were shoveled, SS showed no significant degradation (printed 2-107, PDF 132); RS drag-bed density
   control by test number, Fig. 2-60 (printed 2-102, PDF 127; text printed 2-101, PDF 126).
10. Nominal program: Fig. 4-2 (printed 4-5, PDF 190) gives V_H 1.5, 3, 6, 9, 12, 15 fps and depths 0.5, 1,
    1.5, 2, 3, 4 inch; Fig. 4-3 (same page) gives the number of tests per soil.
11. Normalization check: V_H/sqrt(dg) reproduces with d in feet and g = 32.2 ft/s^2 (run 1-1: 2.43 against
    printed 2.4; run 9-4: 3.16 against printed 3.16). This is my arithmetic, not a printed statement.

## What a simulator user would still have to assume

a. Fill depth of each bed: only the bin's general 11 to 12 inches is printed, and nothing says how many runs
   shared one bed or where across the 3 ft width the pad traveled.
b. Footpad geometry beyond D: no contour dimensions for the 6 inch pad, nothing at all for the 4 inch pad,
   no pad or strut mass. A_ft and A_pt are not printed; they could be backed out of F_D and F_D/A_ft per
   run, but the values obtained differ from run to run, so a simulator user must choose a contour.
c. Pitch freedom: the pad could pivot or be locked. The printed alpha varies from run to run within every
   table (9.3 to 30.3 degrees over all tables), which suggests a free pivot, but no sentence says so for
   these runs.
d. Entry and transient: how the pad was brought to depth d before the run (placed in the bed, plowed in, or
   lowered), the acceleration distance and profile per run, and the transient before the readings at the
   40-inch window.
e. Per-run density: g_p varies run to run while D_r and gamma are usually one value per table, so the bed
   density of an individual run is not printed; mapping g_p to density needs the dynamic penetrometer
   correlations of Section II. The tabulated D_r also differs from the Section II preparation averages
   (soil 4: 0.80 against about 0.7; soil 8: 0.53 against about 0.6; soil 9: 0.69 against about 0.75 to 0.8).
f. Gravity: Earth gravity is implied by the laboratory setting and the normalization check above, but is not
   stated.
g. Units of the derived columns: F_D/A_ft and F_V/A_pt carry no printed unit; their magnitudes fit lb per
   square inch (inference). g_p carries no unit.
h. Unreported channels: side load F_S and roll angle beta were measured (printed 4-4, PDF 189) but are not
   tabulated.
i. Soil properties (friction angle, grading, modulus) are not in these tables; they come from the material
   records of Section II and Volume II.
