A - A 36 cu. ft. wooden storage hopper.

B - Vacuum hose soil intake pipe.

C - Suction pipe; applies partial vacuum in storage hopper and intake pipe.

D - A 25 HP turbine fitted to a 3500 rpm, 30 HP direct drive 440 volt electric motor. The turbine produces a partial vacuum of 6 inches of mercury at a flow of 1000 cu. ft. of air per minute.

E - Pipe for emptying storage hopper into soil placement hopper.

F - Cutoff knife valve for regulating flow of soil from storage hopper.

G - Exhaust pipe.

H - Exhaust port of suction pipe "C."

Although this system allows for rapid soil handling (it will move 400 pounds of soil per minute), it has been found that soil losses through the exhaust system, degradation of soil particles, and hose and turbine impeller damage from abrasion are inordinately high. The addition of more filters and increase of hose diameters (to reduce air velocity) reduced but did not eliminate the problem.

Since the exhaust system tends to remove the fine particles from the soil being handled, this system could not be used for the LSM and RC2 soils.

These soils were handled by shovel. The cinder soils (RS, PS and RSM) that were handled using this system underwent pronounced degradation due to abrasion in passing through the intake hose, and careful periodic sampling of the soils was necessary to keep track of these effects so that their influence could be included in the analysis of the drag and impact tests. Predictably, no significant degradation effects were noted for the SS soil since the silica particles constituting this soil are relatively resistant to breakdown.
