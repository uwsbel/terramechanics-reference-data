## 2.7.5 In-Place Density Measurements

Several types of in-place density measuring devices were used in this study. All of the methods consist of removing a measured weight of soil from the soil bed and measurement of the volume occupied by the soil removed. The weight to volume ratio gives the unit weight or density of the soil. Knowing the minimum-maximum density relationships for the soil, this density measurement allows determination of the relative density of the soil.

The first attempts to measure in-place density of the soil beds utilized the sand cone method*. An amount of soil is carefully removed from the soil bed and weighed. The volume of the hole created is measured by weighing the amount of sand known unit weight required to fill the hole. It was found that although this method worked fairly well in some intermediate and dense soils, gross errors were encountered in loose soils and in the SS soil in any state of density. These errors were eventually traced at least in part to the fact that the weight of the apparatus was causing failure of the sides of the hole.

A modification of the sand cone method using water to measure the volume of the hole created by the soil removal eliminated the slope failure problem, but the correction required to compensate for the folds in the material lining the hole (Saran wrap or thin plastic sheet), and other factors, produced considerable undesirable variation in the results.

A third device for measuring in-place density is a "density scoop" developed by E. T. Selig of Illinois Institute of Technology Research Institute (IITRI). Bendix fabricated their own version of this device and it is shown in Figure 2-59. This device scoops out a fixed volume of soil determined by the radius of the scoop (0.0118 cu.ft.). The weight of the soil scooped out divided by this volume yields a value for unit weight of the soil.

*ASTM Designation D1556-64 (31, pp. 418-422). The equipment used in this study: Soil Test Model No. CN-992, CN-994, and L-770 (33, pp. 47, 49). The sand used was Wedron 4040 (SS soil).
