LM model drop (1/6 scale): SS soil, loose, Detail Correlation Drops

LM model drop (1/6 scale) test on SS soil (White Silica Sand (Wedron 4040-narrowly graded)) at relative density 0; apparatus: modified Langley-Grumman 1/6-scale dynamic model of LM (Figs. 5-1 to 5-3); six-degree-of-freedom drop test rig (Figs. 5-12, 5-13) of the Bendix spacecraft model dynamic drop test laboratory (text 5-22); loading: free-body landing after release from the drop rig; total model weight 68.4 Lbs. (text 5-22); zero angular rates, zero pitch and roll, 45-degree yaw, 2-2 landings (text 5-44); level landing surface, zero slope (text 5-43; Table 5-T2); nominal V_v 10 and V_h 0 ft/s for NASA Test 8, 5 and 7 ft/s for Test 7 (text 5-44, printed teen for ten; Fig. 6-10 gives fps). Recorded: per drop (Table 5-T3): touchdown velocities V_v and V_h, calculated using the measured interval tau between release and ground contact; tau; the bed’s D_r, gamma and phi. Time histories: Section V prints none for Tests 7 and 8; Section VI plots their measured histories against the report’s simulation (Figs. 6-18, 6-19, printed 6-46 to 6-53, PDF 404 to 411), not yet extracted.

The 2 drops below were transcribed from the printed table by two readings made separately, which agree in every cell. No person has yet checked the values of these drops against the scan. The test setup is incomplete: fill depth is not reported.

Data

Download: runs.csv (2 drops, one physical drop per row: every printed value in SI and as printed, and the record of the drop’s time histories where this collection holds one) and runs.json (units, conversion factors, how the table was read).

The table’s legend: V_v is the vertical velocity, V_h the horizontal velocity, tau the time interval between model release and touchdown, D_r the soil relative density, gamma the soil density (printed in Lb./Ft.^3, a unit weight) and phi the internal angle of friction (Table 5-T3, printed 5-45, PDF 325). The velocities “were calculated based on the exact time interval between the instant of model release to the instant of ground contact”, measured from the readings of the accelerometer in the x direction (printed 5-44, PDF 324), into whose output a pulse was injected at release (printed 5-39, PDF 319); no formula is printed. Signs are as printed; the report’s fixed axes point up from the ground and along the horizontal velocity (printed 5-18, PDF 298). Every drop had zero angular rates, zero pitch and roll and a 45-degree yaw (a 2-2 landing) (printed 5-44, PDF 324), on a level bed (printed 5-43, PDF 323). A new bed was prepared for every drop; dynamic penetrometer readings were taken on it after the drop, away from the footpads, and the report points to the correlation of D_r with those readings (Paragraph 2.7.1) and to Section II for the other properties (printed 5-44, PDF 324). Section V prints the time histories of NASA Tests No. 1 and 2 (Figs. 5-22 to 5-39); Section VI plots the measured histories of all eight drops against the report’s computer simulation (Figs. 6-11 and 6-13 to 6-19, printed 6-17 to 6-53, PDF 375 to 411), with the drop conditions again in Fig. 6-10 (printed 6-15, PDF 373).

Our arithmetic, not the report’s, over all 8 drops of the table, against |V_v| = g tau (the speed of a free fall from rest over tau) within the rounding of both printed numbers. With the report’s g for Earth, 32.2 ft/s² (9.81456 m/s²; glossary, printed 2-2, PDF 27), 3 of the 4 vertical drops (V_h 0) and 0 of the 4 drops with a horizontal velocity are consistent with it; with standard gravity, 9.80665 m/s², 4 of 4 and 1 of 4 (NASA Test No. 3). The two values of g differ by 0.08 percent, about the rounding of a printed tau, so at this precision the verdicts depend on the g used. This shows consistency, not the method, which the report does not state.

Time histories of these drops: not yet extracted (the report plots them against its simulation in Section VI).
