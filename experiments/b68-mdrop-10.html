LM model drop (1/6 scale): RS soil, loose, NASA Test No. 1

LM model drop (1/6 scale) test on RS soil (Red Crushed Volcanic Scoria (narrowly graded)) at relative density 0; apparatus: modified 1/6-scale LM model; six-degree-of-freedom drop test rig; loading: free drop; Total Model Weight 68.4 Lbs.; yaw 45 deg (2-2 landing), zero pitch and roll, zero angular rates; level surface (ground slope 0). Recorded: absolute footpad velocity VAP vs time; footpad heading theta vs time; footpad penetration depth vs time; strut No. 1 axial load vs time; strut No. 2 axial load vs time; strut No. 3 axial load vs time; CG vertical acceleration vs time; CG vertical velocity vs time; CG vertical displacement vs time.

The numbers below were digitized from the published figure by two readings made separately; they agree within the stated reading bounds at 196 of the 207 points, and 4 are still under review. They have not yet been checked by a person. The test setup is incomplete: fill depth is not reported.

Data

Peak footpad penetration depth 112.57 mm at 80 ms time; 109.87 mm at 99.8 ms, the end of the record. Axial load, strut No. 3 3.306 N at the end. Axial load, strut No. 2 42.83 N at the end. Axial load, strut No. 1 -20.21 N at the end.

Download: observations.csv (207 points: 0 plotted symbols, 178 samples of the drawn curve; columns x_si, y_si with bounds x_bound_si, y_bound_si; panel 1 (Fig. 5-24): footpad penetration depth in m against time in s; panel 2 (Fig. 5-25): axial load, strut No. 3 in N against time in s; panel 3 (Fig. 5-25): axial load, strut No. 2 in N against time in s; panel 4 (Fig. 5-25): axial load, strut No. 1 in N against time in s; the printed-unit values, the pixel position on the scan and the review status of each point are kept in further columns) and series.json (units, conversion factors, how the points were read).

Reading bounds on these figures (how far our reading of the printed figure may be off, not the uncertainty of the test): typical bound 0.22 ms in time, 551 micrometers in footpad penetration depth (Fig. 5-24) (0.5 percent of the peak), 3.722 N in axial load, strut No. 3 (Fig. 5-25), 3.948 N in axial load, strut No. 2 (Fig. 5-25), 4.001 N in axial load, strut No. 1 (Fig. 5-25). Both independent readings agree within these bounds at 196 of the 207 points; 4 are still under review (hollow in the plot) and 7 were settled against the scan. Check the digitized points against the scan: F5-24, F5-25.

About these values. No. 1 footpad of the 1/6-scale model, NASA Test No. 1. The depth is a computed output, not a direct measurement: the report’s data reduction program took the 13 tape-recorded signals every 1/1000 s and calculated the footpad’s penetration by numerical integration (AM-68-1 Vol. I, Section 5.5.2, printed 5-41, PDF 321). Axial load of the strut, tension positive and compression negative, as the panel’s printed labels give it (the axis title names the sense of the trace’s main excursion); a trace beyond the panel’s 0 line changes sense. On the Strut No. 2 panel the top load label is printed -60; the panel’s own sequence (0, +20, +40) and the (Tension) title make it +60, and both readings anchor that line at +60 lbf. Where the scan has no ink the line is left open: a break in the drawn line at about 0.0138 to 0.0140 s, about 4 px of the page with no ink, the line resuming about 1.5 px to the side.
