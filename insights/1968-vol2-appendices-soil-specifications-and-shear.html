What a simulator user should know about the twelve soils

Three of the sonic moduli in Table 2-3 of Volume I are estimates, not measurements. The modulus of soil 5 (RSM-a loose) was not measured and the specification estimates it at 7800 psi “based on data for the other soils”; soil 10 (LSM intermediate) was not measured and is estimated at 4000 psi from the LSM value at Dr 0.77 (5000 to 6000 psi); soil 12 (RSM-b dense) was not determined and is estimated at about 20,000 psi. Table 2-3 prints all three as if measured. (A-41, PDF 42; A-79, PDF 80; A-93, PDF 94.)

Loose and dense LSM gave essentially the same friction angle, 40 to 45 degrees, because the loose specimen densified under the normal load before it was sheared; the authors could not draw a phi versus Dr curve for LSM and give a band instead, “the estimated range for Dr .40 to 1.00”. The dilation panels of the LSM data sheets show contraction of 270 thousandths of an inch that is nearly constant with shear displacement, which is the compression under the normal load, not a shear response. A model that sets strength from the as-placed density will misread LSM. (A-79, PDF 80; A-88, PDF 89; Figure C-6B, printed C-18, PDF 132; Figure C-21, printed C-43, PDF 157.)

The wide range and confidence limits of RC2 (soil 6) come from segregation of particle sizes within and between beds, not from degradation: RC2 was shoveled, not vacuumed, so it did not break down, but a broadly graded crushed cinder separates as it is handled. The average grain-size curve combines used and unused soil. (A-49, PDF 50.)

The materials susceptible to breakdown under handling were the cinder soils (RS, PS, RSM-a and RSM-b); the silica sand was not altered by degradation or vacuum handling; RC2 and LSM were shoveled. Each specification states the placement procedure (hopper, number of roller passes per layer), which is the only record of how the beds were built: soil 3 two roller passes, soil 4 sixteen, soil 8 eight, soil 9 twenty-four, soil 12 ten per two inch layer; soils 10 and 11 were “rolled two times (one pass back and forth)” and “six times (three passes back and forth)”. The sheets that count passes never say whether a pass is one direction or a round trip, so the number of times the roller crossed a layer is uncertain by a factor of 2 for soils 3, 4, 8, 9 and 12. (A-24, PDF 25; A-32, PDF 33; A-63, PDF 64; A-70, PDF 71; A-79, PDF 80; A-88, PDF 89; A-93, PDF 94.)

Every specification value, with its page, is in data/materials/specifications.csv, and the generated qa/specifications/table23-vs-appendix-a.md sets each Table 2-3 value beside it: 26 cells differ. The Table 2-3 unit weights reproduce the stated relative densities on the specification density charts within 0.04, except loose SS, whose 94.7 pcf lies below the chart’s minimum density and gives Dr -0.05 (Claude’s arithmetic with Vol. I equation 2-2).

The mixtures are specified by weight: LSM is RC2 cinders and a kaolin-type clay 1:1, RSM-a is cinders and crushed marble 5:7, and RSM-b is cinders and crushed marble 1:3. RSM-a and RSM-b are therefore different materials, not two densities of one material. (A-34, PDF 35; A-73, PDF 74; A-82, PDF 83; A-90, PDF 91.)

The specification text and Table 2-3 disagree on two friction angles: the text gives about 38.3 degrees for soil 11 and about 37 for soil 12, the table 42 and 48; the Appendix A and C curves support the table for soil 12 (about 50 at Dr 1.0) and the discrepancy for soil 11 is unexplained. Soil 12’s placement is also printed differently in the two volumes (10 roller passes per two inch layer in A-93; twenty-four rolls in Volume I printed 2-113). (A-84, PDF 85; A-93, PDF 94.)

The transcription of soils 1 to 6 found more disagreements with Table 2-3. The text gives about 43.9 degrees for soil 3 and 47.9 for soil 4, the table 44.5 and 47.3. Table 2-3 prints one gradation (mean size 1.8 mm, uniformity 1.7, coarseness 2.7 mm) for all four RS and PS rows, while the Appendix A grain size charts give RS a mean size of 2.2 mm and coarseness of 2.8 mm, and PS a uniformity of 1.6. For RC2 the chart prints an average uniformity of “2 5” (range 15-25) and coarseness of 5.0 mm, the table 17.0 and 4.7 mm. Which source is right is not settled by the pages; the measured curves themselves (step 3b) can check it. A user setting up a bed from Table 2-3 should know the table and the specifications differ. (A-6, PDF 7; A-13, PDF 14; A-20, PDF 21; A-28, PDF 29; A-46, PDF 47; Vol. I Table 2-3, printed 2-16, PDF 41; qa/pages-vol2-ab-b2/review.md.)

The silica sand follows the same pattern. The text gives about 29.8 degrees for soil 7, the table 29. The three SS grain size charts give a uniformity of 1.5 and a coarseness of 0.6 mm (range 0.58-0.62), the table 1.3 and 0.52 mm. The table’s unit weight for loose SS, 94.7 pcf, is below every minimum density on the SS density chart (95.0 to 95.8 pcf). (A-52, PDF 53; A-53, PDF 54; A-54, PDF 55; qa/pages-vol2-ab-b3/review.md.)

What the shear data are

Appendix C holds 85 single direct shear tests on 15 soil-and-density series, as data sheets of shear stress and dilation versus shear displacement with the normal stress and unit weight of each test in the legend, plus hand-drawn and computer-fitted failure envelopes. Only one of the 15 series (RS at Dr .50 to .57, Figure C-1B) appears in Volume I (Figure 2-21). The computer fits print “Avg. Density”, “Angle of Friction”, “Tan Theta” and “Cohesion”, and several of the cohesion intercepts are large (700 psf for dense RS) or negative (LSM loose), which says the linear fit is being forced through data that are not linear in normal stress; the hand-drawn envelopes on C-2 to C-21 are the authors’ own reading. (C-1, PDF 115; Figures C-8 to C-15, printed C-24 to C-37, PDF 138 to 151.)

Three of the SS series (Dr .30, .48 and .77) were not run in this study; they are credited to the Bendix interim report MM-65-2 on lunar landing dynamics (as printed on Figure C-5A), which is therefore a source in its own right for silica sand strength. The RSM-b tests (Figure C-7) were run on a different apparatus with loads in psi and no dilation record. (C-1, PDF 115; Figure C-5A, printed C-15, PDF 129; Figures C-7A to C-7C, printed C-21 to C-23, PDF 135 to 137.)

What the sonic modulus data are

The IITRI measurements (Appendix B) give E = ρc² from a stress-wave velocity in a triaxial specimen confined by partial vacuum, at confining pressures from 0 to about 10 psi, loose and dense, for RS, PS, SS, RC and LSM; there is no RSM measurement. The loose states are Dr 0.30, 0.22, 0.26 and 0.02, not zero. The report calls the LSM results questionable because of a confining pressure gradient in the specimen, and notes that E = ρc² neglects the Poisson factor f(nu), an error under 10 percent for nu below 0.20. (IITRI report M6175, Figures 3 to 8, PDF 109 to 114.)

The “elastic modulus” of Table 2-3 is therefore a small-strain wave modulus, and IITRI says so plainly: moduli from static triaxial tests “will generally differ by an order of magnitude” from wave-velocity values, and dynamic stress-strain curves give values that “may differ by as much as a factor of 4”. The stress wave peaked below 2 psi; loose specimens were poured through a slotted funnel and dense ones vibrated. A simulation that takes E from Table 2-3 is using a wave modulus, not a static one. Under IITRI’s own equation (2), E = ρc² f(nu) with f(nu) at most 1, so ρc² is an upper bound on E for the confined case (Claude’s reading of the printed equations). (Printed 1 to 3, PDF 104 to 106.)

Table 2-3’s moduli at Dr 0, 0.45 and 0.80 were not measured at those densities; IITRI tested RS only at Dr 0.30 and 0.99. Read by eye at 4 psi, the two RS curves give about 7.7 and 12.5 kips/in.², and a straight line in Dr through them gives 5.6, 8.7 and 11.2 at Dr 0, 0.45 and 0.80, close to the table’s 5,500, 8,600 and 10,900 psi. The table looks interpolated and extrapolated in Dr from the IITRI curves; this is an eye reading by Claude, to be confirmed when the curves are read (roadmap step 3b). (Figure 3, printed 6, PDF 109; Vol. I Table 2-3, printed 2-16, PDF 41.)

A caution on the appendix pages themselves

Two print slips matter to anyone reading the IITRI curves. On Figure 3 (RS) the y tick label between 9 and 7 is printed “3” where 8 belongs, so an axis calibration must not use it. On Figure 5 (SS) the dense label reads “o.84” with a lowercase o for the zero, and the loose label’s “0.” is broken (the value is 0.26). Figure 7 (LSM) includes a specimen at Dr = 1.04, denser than the maximum-density test. (Printed 6, 8 and 10, PDF 109, 111 and 113.)

The Appendix A figures are printed four times per material under new figure numbers (soils 1, 3, 4 share RS; 7, 8, 9 share SS; 10, 11 share LSM), and the “RSM” photomicrograph under soil 12 is the soil 5 photograph reused. The Volcalite chemical analysis is reproduced with two different typesetting slips (A-72, A-81): one gives calcium oxide the magnesium values and drops magnesium, the other gives magnesium oxide the sodium values and drops sodium, and both keep the total 99.25 that their columns no longer reach. The two printings of the clay analysis differ in iron oxide (1.1% on A-72, which matches the 100.0% total; 1.2% on A-81). An agent quoting a value from a specification should quote the first printing of that table (A-2, A-10, A-50, A-72 for the clay) and treat the repeats as copies. (A-72, PDF 73; A-81, PDF 82; A-91, PDF 92.)
